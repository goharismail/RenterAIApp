How to Calculate Prorated Rent: Formula, Examples & Tips

✍️ RenterAI Editorial Team 📅 October 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 🧮 Includes a free calculator

Moving in on the 15th? Moving out on the 10th? Most leases run from the first of the month to the last, so a mid-month date means you pay for only part of a month. That partial payment is called prorated rent, and the math is simple once you see it laid out.

What Is Prorated Rent?

Prorated rent is rent charged for part of a rental period instead of the whole period. It usually comes up in three situations: your lease starts in the middle of a month, your lease ends in the middle of a month, or you move in early or stay a few extra days. Instead of paying a full month, you pay for the days you actually have the home.

The Prorated Rent Formula

There are two steps:

1. Daily rent = monthly rent ÷ number of days in the month
2. Prorated rent = daily rent × number of days you occupy the home

The only part that varies is how the "number of days in the month" is counted, which we cover below.

Example 1: Moving In Mid-Month

Say your rent is $1,800 and you move in on March 20. March has 31 days, and counting March 20 through March 31 gives you 12 days.

StepCalculationResult
Daily rent$1,800 ÷ 31$58.06
Days you occupyMarch 20 to March 3112 days
Prorated rent$1,800 × 12 ÷ 31$696.77

On April 1 you would then pay the first full month of rent, so your first two payments are the prorated March amount and the full April rent.

Example 2: Moving Out Mid-Month

Say rent is $1,500 and your last day is June 10. June has 30 days, so the daily rent is $1,500 ÷ 30 = $50.00, and 10 days of June gives you $50.00 × 10 = $500.00.

Three Ways Landlords Count Days

Not every lease uses the actual number of days in the month. Using Example 1 ($1,800 rent, 12 days in March):

MethodHow it worksResult
Actual days in the month$1,800 × 12 ÷ 31$696.77
30-day month$1,800 × 12 ÷ 30$720.00
365-day year$1,800 × 12 ÷ 365 × 12 days$710.14

The difference between methods is about $23 here. It is worth knowing which one your lease uses before you compare numbers.

Does the Move-In Day Count?

Many leases count the move-in day as a rented day, but some start charging the next day. In Example 1, not counting March 20 would make it 11 days instead of 12 and lower the prorated rent by about $58. Check the exact wording in your lease or ask your landlord how they count.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong month length. February, April and 31-day months give different daily rates.
  • Miscounting days. Count both the first and last day if your lease counts both.
  • Forgetting other charges. A security deposit, pet fee or application fee is usually separate from prorated rent. Our Moving and Move-In Cost Calculator helps you add everything up.
  • Assuming it is automatic. Some landlords prorate and some do not, so confirm before you sign.

Questions to Ask Your Landlord

  • How is the first (or last) partial month calculated?
  • Does the move-in or move-out day count?
  • When is the next full rent payment due?
  • Are deposits and fees charged in full?

Get the answers and the final amount in writing, and keep a copy with your lease.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate prorated rent?+

Divide the monthly rent by the number of days in the month to get a daily rate, then multiply by the number of days you will occupy the home. For $1,800 rent and 12 days in a 31-day month, that is $1,800 ÷ 31 × 12 = $696.77.

Is prorated rent calculated on 30 days or the actual days in the month?+

It depends on the lease. Many use the actual days in the month, while some use a 30-day month or a 365-day year. The result can differ by a few dollars to a few dozen, so check which method your lease uses.

Do I have to pay prorated rent?+

If you move in or out in the middle of a month, you generally pay for the days you have the home, but how it is handled depends on your lease and landlord. Ask how the first or last month will be calculated and get it in writing.

Is a security deposit prorated?+

Deposits are usually charged in full rather than prorated, but your lease controls. Check your lease and ask your landlord.
