What Is a Roommate Agreement?

A roommate agreement is a written document that helps people sharing a rental home or apartment agree on household responsibilities. It can outline how roommates will divide rent and utilities, handle cleaning, manage guests, follow quiet hours, and deal with shared household issues.

A roommate agreement is generally different from the lease you have with your landlord. Your lease and local rules may contain requirements that are not covered by a roommate agreement.

What Should a Roommate Agreement Include?

A useful roommate agreement can cover the practical rules that affect everyday life in a shared home.

  • Monthly rent and each roommate's share
  • How utilities and household expenses are divided
  • Cleaning responsibilities and schedules
  • Guest and overnight visitor rules
  • Quiet hours and shared-space expectations
  • Pet and household policies
  • Move-out procedures and communication
  • How roommates will handle disagreements

Why Use a Roommate Agreement?

Discussing household expectations before problems happen can make shared living easier. Putting agreements in writing can also give roommates a clear reference when questions arise about rent, chores, guests, or shared expenses.

How to Create a Roommate Agreement

Start by identifying everyone who will share the home. Then agree on rent, utilities, household responsibilities, guests, cleaning, quiet hours, and other important expectations.

Roommate Agreement vs. Lease Agreement

A lease agreement is typically the contract governing the rental relationship with the landlord or property owner. A roommate agreement focuses on arrangements between people sharing the rental.

A roommate agreement should not be treated as a replacement for your lease. Roommates should review both documents and make sure their household arrangements do not conflict with applicable lease terms or property rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a roommate agreement?

A roommate agreement is a written arrangement between people who share a rental home. It can explain how they will divide expenses and handle household responsibilities and expectations.

Is a roommate agreement the same as a lease?

No. A roommate agreement is generally focused on arrangements between roommates, while a lease governs the rental relationship with the landlord or property owner. Your lease remains important even if you create a roommate agreement.

What should I put in a roommate agreement?

Common topics include rent, utilities, cleaning, guests, quiet hours, shared spaces, pets, household supplies, move-out arrangements, and procedures for resolving disagreements.

Can roommates split rent differently?

Yes. Roommates can agree on a method that works for their household, such as an equal split, a room-size-based split, or another arrangement. The exact amounts should be agreed upon by everyone involved.

Can I customize this roommate agreement?

Yes. You can select the sections you want to include and add additional details about your household. The generated document should be reviewed and edited before the roommates use it.

Does a roommate agreement replace my lease?

No. A roommate agreement should not be used as a replacement for the rental lease. Review both documents and verify any questions about landlord requirements or local law with an appropriate housing or legal resource.

Can I use a roommate agreement template?

A template can provide a useful starting point, but every household has different arrangements. Customize the agreement to reflect your actual rent, expenses, responsibilities, and household rules.
