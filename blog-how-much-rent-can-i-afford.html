How Much Rent Can I Afford? Rent by Income Guide (2026)

✍️ RenterAI Editorial Team 📅 October 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read 🧮 Includes a free calculator

"How much rent can I afford?" is the first question in any apartment search, and the answer is not a single number. Rules of thumb give you a starting range, and your own budget decides where in that range you land. This guide explains the common rules, shows rent by income, and points out what the rules leave out.

The 30% Rule

The most common guideline is to spend no more than about 30% of your gross (before-tax) income on housing. The 30% figure is also widely used in U.S. housing policy as a benchmark for when housing costs become a heavy burden. To use it, multiply your gross monthly income by 0.30.

The 3× Rent Rule and the 40× Rule

Some landlords use rules like these to screen applicants. The 3× rule asks for monthly gross income of at least three times the rent. The 40× rule asks for annual gross income of at least 40 times the monthly rent. Requirements differ by landlord, building and city, so check the requirements of the places you apply to.

A Quick Budget Check: 50/30/20

Another widely used framework splits take-home pay into roughly 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt repayment. Rent, utilities, groceries and transportation are all "needs," so if rent and utilities alone use most of that 50%, your budget will feel tight. It is a framework for thinking, not a requirement.

What the Rules Leave Out

  • Debt. Student loans, car payments and credit cards reduce what is left for rent. Enter your monthly debt payments in the calculator to see rent plus debt as a share of income.
  • Total housing cost. Utilities, renters insurance, parking and fees add to the rent. Our renters insurance guide explains one common extra.
  • Where you live. In high-cost cities, rents may exceed 30% of income for many households.
  • Take-home pay. Rules use gross income, but you pay rent from take-home pay, which depends on taxes and benefits.
  • Move-in costs. A deposit, first month and moving costs are paid up front. See the Moving and Move-In Cost Calculator.

Ways to Lower Your Rent Share

  • Share a place. Splitting rent can bring your share down. Use the Rent Split Calculator and read how to split rent fairly.
  • Compare total costs. A lower rent with high utilities or parking fees may cost more than it looks.
  • Read the lease. Know about fees and increases before you sign. See how to read a lease.

How to Decide

Start with the range from the 30% rule, then adjust for your debt, savings goals and city. If a place sits above the range, it does not mean you cannot afford it, but it may leave less room for savings and surprises. If it sits well below, you may have more flexibility for other goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much rent can I afford on $50,000 a year?+

Using the 30% rule, $50,000 a year is about $4,167 a month before taxes, so rent of about $1,250 a month. At 25% it is about $1,042, and under the 3× rule it is about $1,389. Your own budget may point to a different number.

What is the 30% rule for rent?+

It is a guideline that says to spend no more than about 30% of your gross monthly income on housing. It is a rule of thumb and not a requirement.

Is the 40× rule the same as the 30% rule?+

Yes, mathematically. Requiring annual income of 40 times the monthly rent means rent is at most 1/40 of annual income, which is the same as 30% of monthly income.

Should I use gross or net income to calculate rent?+

Most rent rules, including the 30% rule and the 3× rule, use gross income. Looking at rent as a share of take-home pay is also useful because that is where you pay rent from.

Does the 30% rule include utilities?+

It is usually applied to total housing costs, so utilities and renters insurance can count toward it. Some people apply it to rent alone. Pick one approach and use it consistently when you compare places.
