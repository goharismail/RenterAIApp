📆 Your Prorated Rent

What Is Prorated Rent?

Prorated rent is rent charged for only part of a rental period. It usually comes up when you move in or out in the middle of a month. Instead of paying a full month, you pay for the days you actually have the home.

How to Calculate Prorated Rent

The basic formula has two steps:

  1. Daily rent = monthly rent ÷ number of days in the month
  2. Prorated rent = daily rent × number of days you occupy the home

Example: Rent is $1,500 and you move in on October 15. October has 31 days, so daily rent is $1,500 ÷ 31 = $48.39. From October 15 to October 31 is 17 days (counting the move-in day), so prorated rent is 17 × $48.39 = $822.58.

Day-Count Methods Compared

Leases do not all calculate the daily rate the same way. For the example above:

MethodHow it worksResult
Actual days in the month$1,500 × 17 ÷ 31$822.58
30-day month$1,500 × 17 ÷ 30$850.00
365-day year$1,500 × 12 ÷ 365 × 17$838.36

Many leases use the actual number of days in the month, but some use a 30-day month or a 365-day year. Check which one your lease or landlord uses, since the amounts can differ by tens of dollars.

Do Landlords Have to Prorate Rent?

That depends on your lease, your landlord's policy and local rules. Some landlords prorate the first month, some ask you to pay a full month, and some set the lease start date to the first of the month so no proration is needed. If you are unsure, ask your landlord how the first month will be calculated and get the amount in writing before you sign.

Is the Move-In Day Counted?

It depends on the lease. Counting the move-in day is common, but some leases start charging the following day. Use the checkbox in the calculator to match your lease and see how a one-day difference changes the result.

Tips for Mid-Month Moves

  • Ask for the prorated amount and the due date of the next full payment in writing.
  • Check whether deposits and one-time fees are charged in full. They are often not prorated, but your lease controls.
  • For a move-out, confirm the last day you are responsible for rent and when your keys must be returned.
  • Compare the proration with the cost of choosing a start date on the first of the month.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate prorated rent?

Divide the monthly rent by the number of days in the month to get a daily rate, then multiply by the number of days you will occupy the home. For $1,500 rent and 17 days in a 31-day month, that is $1,500 ÷ 31 × 17 = $822.58.

Is the move-in day counted in prorated rent?

It depends on your lease. Many leases count the move-in day as a rented day, but some start charging the next day. Use the checkbox to compare both.

How do I prorate rent when I move out mid-month?

Use the same formula with the days from the first of the month through your move-out date. Choose the Move-out option in the calculator and enter your last day.

Do landlords have to prorate rent?

Not always. It depends on your lease, your landlord's policy and local rules. Ask how the first or last month will be handled and get it in writing.

Is a security deposit prorated?

Deposits are usually charged in full rather than prorated, but your lease controls. Enter a deposit in the other charges field to see it added to your total.

Can I prorate rent across two months?

Yes. Choose Custom date range. The calculator splits the range by month and prorates each month separately when you use the actual-days method.
