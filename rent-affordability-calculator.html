🏠 Your Rent Ranges

What Is the 30% Rule for Rent?

The 30% rule is a common guideline that says you should spend no more than about 30% of your gross (before-tax) income on housing. The 30% figure is also a widely used benchmark in U.S. housing policy for judging whether housing costs are a heavy burden. It is a rule of thumb, not a legal requirement, and it does not fit every budget.

Other Rules Landlords and Renters Use

  • 3× rent rule: some landlords ask for monthly gross income of at least three times the rent, which means rent of about one-third of income.
  • 40× rule: some landlords, especially in some large cities, ask for annual gross income of at least 40 times the monthly rent.
  • Conservative budgeting: many people aim for 25% or less to leave room for savings, debt and emergencies.

Screening requirements vary by landlord, city and building, so check the requirements of the places you are applying to.

When a Rule of Thumb Does Not Fit

  • High-cost cities: rents may be above 30% of income for many households.
  • Debt: student loans, car payments and credit cards can leave less room than the percentage suggests.
  • Total housing cost: utilities, renters insurance, parking and fees add to your rent.
  • Roommates: splitting rent changes your share. Try the Rent Split Calculator.

Gross Income vs. Take-Home Pay

Most rent guidelines use gross income (before taxes). Because you pay rent from take-home pay, it also helps to look at rent as a share of what actually lands in your account. Enter your take-home pay above to see both numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much rent can I afford on $50,000 a year?

Using the 30% rule, $50,000 a year is about $4,167 a month before taxes, so rent of about $1,250 a month. At 25% it is about $1,042, and under the 3× rule it is about $1,389. Your own budget may point to a different number.

What is the 40x rule for renting?

Some landlords ask for annual gross income of at least 40 times the monthly rent. For $1,500 rent, that is $60,000 a year. Requirements vary by landlord and city.

Should rent be based on gross or take-home pay?

Most guidelines, including the 30% rule and the 3× rule, use gross income. Looking at rent as a share of take-home pay is also useful, because that is the money you pay it from.

Does the rent guideline include utilities?

The 30% rule is usually applied to total housing costs, so utilities and renters insurance can count toward it. Some people apply it to rent alone. Decide which you are using before you compare.

What if I have roommates?

Use your share of the rent. A roommate's income can also change what a landlord requires, so ask how the property screens multiple applicants.

How do I enter hourly pay?

Choose Per hour, enter your hourly rate and your weekly hours. The calculator converts it to monthly income using 52 weeks per year.
