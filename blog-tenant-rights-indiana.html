Tenant Rights in Indiana 2026: Complete Renter's Guide

🔄 Corrected October 2026 ✍️ RenterAI Editorial Team 📅 June 2026 ⏱️ 11 min read 📍 Indiana — Indianapolis, Fort Wayne, Evansville ✅ Updated for 2026

If you rent month-to-month in Indiana, either side generally ends the tenancy with one month's written notice (IC 32-31-1-1). A year-to-year tenancy needs three months' notice (IC 32-31-1-3). Because the rules depend on the type of tenancy you have, read your lease and the current statute before you give or accept a notice.

Most Indiana renters do not know this. Most landlords do too — or at least hope you do not. It is one of several Indiana-specific rules that can work strongly in a renter's favor when they come up, and it is a good example of how Indiana tenant law rewards renters who actually know what it says.

Indiana is generally considered a landlord-friendly state. There is no rent control, no deposit cap, and the eviction process moves relatively quickly for non-payment cases. Indiana does set a 45-day deadline for returning deposits (IC 32-31-3-12). Combined with habitability protections that include a legal right to air conditioning, Indiana renters have more legal tools available than most people realize.

⚖️ Indiana Law — IC Title 32, Article 31

Indiana's landlord-tenant law is in Indiana Code Title 32, Article 31 — Landlord-Tenant Relations. Key sections: IC 32-31-3 (security deposits), IC 32-31-8 (landlord duties including habitability), IC 32-31-7 (eviction procedures), IC 32-31-5 (lease terms and termination). Read the current statute text on the Indiana General Assembly website before relying on any detail here.

Ending a Month-to-Month Tenancy in Indiana

Under IC 32-31-1-1, a month-to-month tenancy (a tenancy at will) can generally be ended by one month's written notice. A year-to-year tenancy needs at least three months' notice before the end of the year (IC 32-31-1-3). A fixed-term lease simply ends on the date stated in the lease unless it says otherwise.

Indiana has no statewide "just cause" requirement for ending a month-to-month tenancy, so a landlord does not have to give a reason if the notice is proper. Notice should be in writing and delivered in a way the statute allows (IC 32-31-1-9). If you receive a notice and are not sure whether it is valid, do not ignore it. Ask a legal aid organization or your court's self-help center to review it, because staying past a valid notice can lead to an eviction case.

✅ Moving out of a month-to-month rental? Give your landlord written notice at least one full rental period (usually 30 days) before you leave, keep a copy, and check your lease for any longer notice period it requires.

Security Deposit Rights in Indiana

Indiana's deposit rules are in IC 32-31-3. Under IC 32-31-3-12, the landlord has 45 days to return the deposit or provide an itemized list of deductions, counted from the end of the tenancy and the tenant's delivery of a forwarding address.

After you vacate, your landlord has 45 days to either return your full deposit or provide a written itemized statement of deductions along with any remaining balance. The itemized statement must explain each deduction specifically — vague descriptions like "cleaning and repairs" without specifics may not be sufficient under Indiana's requirements.

Indiana has no statutory cap on how much a landlord can charge as a security deposit. In practice, most Indiana landlords charge one to two months rent. If you are being asked for an unusually large deposit, negotiate — there is no law preventing you from pushing back, and there is also no law preventing a landlord from asking for more than two months.

🚩 45 days passed with no deposit? Consider sending a written request first. If that does not work, small claims court is one option. Time limits for filing differ by claim type, so check with a legal aid organization or the court's self-help center before waiting too long.

Application Fees

Indiana's landlord-tenant statute does not set a statewide limit on application fees, so landlords can generally charge one. Ask what the fee covers (such as a credit or background check) and whether any part is refundable before you pay, and keep a receipt.

Keep a copy of any application and receipt in case there is a later dispute about fees or screening.

Air Conditioning — A Legal Requirement in Indiana

Indiana requires landlords to provide working air conditioning as part of a habitable rental unit. This is more than just good practice — it is part of Indiana's habitability standards under IC 32-31-8-5. Indiana summers can push past 90°F with high humidity, making functioning AC a genuine health and safety issue, not a luxury.

If your AC breaks down and your landlord does not address it within a reasonable time after written notice, you have the right to seek repairs and potentially deduct the cost from rent. Document the problem with photos and timestamps, notify your landlord in writing immediately, and give them approximately 14 days to respond before escalating.

Right to a Habitable Home

Indiana landlords must maintain rental properties in compliance with all applicable housing and health codes and keep them fit for human habitation. This means working heat, working plumbing with hot water, functioning electrical systems, a structurally sound building, working smoke detectors, and freedom from pest infestations — in addition to working AC.

If your landlord fails to make required repairs after written notice, Indiana gives them a reasonable time to respond — courts generally interpret this as approximately 14 days for most repairs, less for urgent health and safety issues. If repairs are not made, you have options including repair-and-deduct, rent withholding, and in serious cases lease termination without penalty. Always notify in writing first and keep copies of every communication.

Eviction Laws in Indiana

Indiana's eviction process is relatively streamlined, particularly for non-payment cases. The 10-day notice period for non-payment is shorter than most states, and once the case is filed in small claims court, hearings are typically scheduled within a few weeks.

Notice Periods

For non-payment of rent, your landlord must give you a written 10-day notice to pay all rent owed or vacate. Pay in full within those 10 days and the eviction process stops. For lease violations and holdover tenants — staying after a lease ends without a new agreement — the notice period is 30 days. For criminal activity or serious property damage, landlords can act on a shorter timeline.

An important protection: Indiana law states that landlords cannot evict solely for non-payment of late fees while base rent is paid on time. If you paid your rent but not a late fee, that alone is not sufficient grounds to file for eviction in Indiana.

CARES Act Properties — 30-Day Minimum

If you live in a property that received federal CARES Act assistance — many larger apartment complexes and HUD-assisted properties — you are entitled to a minimum 30-day notice before any eviction filing for non-payment, regardless of what state law says. This federal protection applies even though the rest of the CARES Act's eviction moratorium expired. If you live in a large complex, check whether it is covered.

🚩 Self-Help Eviction is Illegal. Indiana landlords cannot change locks, remove belongings, shut off utilities, or physically remove you without a court order. These are illegal self-help evictions. If your landlord attempts this, call the police, document everything, and contact Indiana Legal Services immediately — you have the right to remain in possession until a court orders otherwise.

Retaliation Protection

Under IC 32-31-8-6, Indiana landlords cannot evict you, raise your rent, or reduce services in retaliation for reporting housing code violations, requesting repairs, or exercising any legal right. If your landlord takes adverse action shortly after protected activity, document the timeline carefully — this evidence is critical in retaliation claims.

Property Ownership Transfer

If your rental changes owners, Indiana's deposit statute (IC 32-31-3) requires the transfer of the deposit and written notice to tenants. Keep your lease and deposit receipt so you can show who is responsible for your deposit.

No Rent Control in Indiana

Indiana has no statewide rent control and does not permit cities or counties to enact rent stabilization. Landlords can raise rent by any amount between lease terms. Check your lease and the current statute for any notice rules that apply to a rent increase. During a fixed-term lease, rent cannot be raised unless the lease specifically allows it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are my rights as a tenant in Indiana?+

Indiana renters are protected under IC Title 32, Article 31, including habitability duties, a 45-day deadline to return security deposits with an itemized list of deductions, and notice rules for eviction and ending a tenancy. Indiana has no statewide rent control. Check the current statute text for details that apply to you.

How long does my landlord have to return my deposit in Indiana?+

45 days after you vacate (IC 32-31-3-12). Along with the deposit, they must provide a written itemized statement of any deductions. Indiana has a 2-year statute of limitations for deposit claims from the end of the lease. If 45 days pass with no deposit and no communication, file in Indiana small claims court.

Can Indiana landlords charge application fees?+

Indiana's landlord-tenant statute does not set a statewide limit on application fees. Ask what the fee covers and whether any part is refundable, and keep a receipt. Local rules or your lease may add details.

What is the notice period to end a month-to-month lease in Indiana?+

Generally one month's written notice for a month-to-month tenancy (IC 32-31-1-1). A year-to-year tenancy needs three months' notice (IC 32-31-1-3). Check your lease and the current statute, and ask a legal aid organization if you are unsure about a notice you received.

How many days notice before eviction in Indiana?+

For non-payment: 10-day written notice to pay or vacate. For lease violations/holdover: 30 days. Landlords cannot evict solely for unpaid late fees when base rent is current. CARES Act properties get a 30-day minimum for non-payment regardless of state law. After notice, landlords must file in small claims court — only a court order and officer can physically remove you.

Is there rent control in Indiana?+

No. Indiana has no statewide rent control and cities cannot enact it (IC 32-31-1-20). Landlords can generally raise rent between lease terms, but they cannot raise it during a fixed-term lease unless the lease allows it, and retaliatory increases are restricted. Check your lease and current law for notice rules.

Does Indiana require air conditioning in rental properties?+

Yes. Indiana's habitability standards require landlords to provide working air conditioning. If your AC breaks and your landlord does not address it within a reasonable time after written notice, you have remedies including repair-and-deduct. Document the failure with photos and timestamps, notify your landlord in writing immediately, and give approximately 14 days before escalating.
